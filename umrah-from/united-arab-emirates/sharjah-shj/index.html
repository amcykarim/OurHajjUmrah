Data and freshness policy

Airport geography comes from the user-provided OurAirports dataset. Country fields come from the Phase 1A.3 launch dataset. Changing Saudi requirements and religious guidance remain separate reviewed layers. This data architecture is what allows international scale without manufacturing unsupported facts.

Practical itinerary checklist

Before treating the departure plan as complete, check that the passenger name matches the travel document, the departure airport and terminal information match the actual booking, connection times are understood, baggage rules have been checked with the carrier, and the Saudi arrival airport matches the onward plan. These are planning checks rather than live claims stored by OurHajjUmrah.

Keep copies of important travel information available during the journey and leave enough time to handle airport procedures. If the itinerary contains a connection, review where baggage is collected or transferred and whether any transit requirements apply to the traveler's circumstances. Those details belong to the real booking and current official guidance.

How this departure guide stays useful

Airport names, codes, municipalities and coordinates are relatively stable, while airline schedules, fares, entry rules and operating procedures can change quickly. The generator therefore builds the permanent page around stable geography and links travelers toward current sources for volatile information. This reduces the chance that a useful airport guide becomes misleading simply because a schedule or rule changed.

As OurHajjUmrah grows, destination guides, checklists and reviewed pilgrimage instructions can be updated centrally. Departure pages can then point to those shared resources without copying large blocks of potentially outdated information into every city page.
